#### Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment**: The page no longer reads as a generic SaaS landing page, but it still has visible AI-design residue. The strongest tell is the move toward a warm near-white pearl background plus Cormorant Garamond display type, which is explicitly in the brand reference's reflex-reject list. The collection section also falls back into a same-size card grid, and the repeated small uppercase labels recreate the eyebrow pattern the brief wanted to avoid.

**Deterministic scan**: `detect.mjs --json src/App.jsx` returned `[]`, so no bundled detector findings were reported. This is useful, but it does not catch all brand-register issues, especially font-lane reflexes and section rhythm.

**Visual overlays**: Browser overlay inspection was not available because this environment exposes no browser automation tool. No reliable user-visible overlay was created.

#### Overall Impression

The direction is much stronger than before: real assets, real logo, real contact paths, and a deliberate scroll narrative. The biggest opportunity is to make the art direction feel owned by AREA LRMQ rather than like an upscale landing-page generator: less decorative serif editorial, fewer repeated cards, clearer chapter controls, and a stronger production path for mobile and reduced motion.

#### What's Working

- The real logo materially improves credibility. It anchors the nav, hero, Vision, and Contact sections, and it makes the page feel tied to a real business instead of a placeholder studio.
- The Vision concept is the most distinctive interaction. Sketch video followed by a comparison slider is specific to renovation decision-making and supports the product story.
- Contact is finally conversion-ready in structure. WhatsApp, phone, Instagram, address, and maps are visible, concrete, and tied to the real business data.

#### Priority Issues

**[P1] The current typography choice conflicts with the brand reference**

**Why it matters**: `Cormorant Garamond` is explicitly listed as a reflex-reject font in `reference/brand.md`. It pushes the page toward a saturated editorial-premium lane instead of a more owned AREA LRMQ identity. The logo itself already has elegant serif contrast, so the page can echo that without using a default AI-premium serif.

**Fix**: Replace Cormorant with a sharper, less saturated display choice, or use a single-family system with a custom-feeling high-contrast display treatment. Keep Manrope only if it supports the logo rather than competing with it.

**Suggested command**: `/impeccable typeset src/App.jsx`

**[P1] Reduced-motion and desktop control are not truly accessible**

**Why it matters**: CSS reduces transition durations, but React still renders a fixed desktop stage and captures wheel/key navigation. Users who request reduced motion should not be locked into a theatrical chapter controller with auto-playing videos and forced pacing.

**Fix**: Detect `prefers-reduced-motion` in `useNarrativeScroll`; if active, render the mobile/native-flow layout on all viewport sizes, show video controls, and avoid blocking advancement on video playback.

**Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden src/App.jsx`

**[P1] The collection section still reads like a card grid**

**Why it matters**: Four same-sized cards with image, label, title, and copy are exactly the kind of landing-page reflex the brief rejects. The section should feel curated, like materials being selected on a worktable, not like product category tiles.

**Fix**: Break the grid. Use one large hero material panel, two smaller stacked selections, and one text-only recommendation note, or create a horizontal product bench with staggered reveals.

**Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout src/App.jsx`

**[P2] Chapter status is under-explained**

**Why it matters**: The experience relies on scroll gestures, step reveals, video blocking, and a continuous renovation scrub. The only persistent status is small dots. A user can feel trapped or unsure whether the page is waiting, playing, or ready for the next gesture.

**Fix**: Add a compact chapter rail with labels or a current chapter caption. For video-blocked moments, show a clear skip or wait state.

**Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify src/App.jsx`

**[P2] Mobile is a fallback, not a crafted experience**

**Why it matters**: Mobile sections reuse desktop chapter components with `h-full` internal layout and heavy visual structures. That can create cramped sections, awkward hero stacking, and content that feels adapted rather than designed.

**Fix**: Give mobile its own simplified compositions: image first, short copy, direct CTA, native video controls, no hidden reveal dependencies.

**Suggested command**: `/impeccable adapt src/App.jsx`

#### Persona Red Flags

**Maria, homeowner comparing renovation shops on her phone**: She sees premium visuals, but the mobile page may feel long and heavy because desktop components are reused. If she wants quick proof of location, products, and contact, the narrative can slow her down.

**Javier, detail-oriented client planning a bathroom renovation on desktop**: The Vision and Reforma interactions are useful, but he may not understand why scrolling sometimes reveals copy and sometimes scrubs video. Missing labels and skip affordances reduce trust.

**Ana, motion-sensitive visitor**: Reduced-motion preferences are not respected at the React layout level. She still gets the fixed stage and scroll choreography, which can make the page uncomfortable or unusable.

#### Minor Observations

- Hero heading uses `lg:text-[7rem]`, above the 6rem ceiling in the design guidance and risky for overflow.
- Several Spanish strings are missing accents because the source is ASCII-only: `bano`, `asesoria`, `coleccion`, `griferia`, `armonia`. If the production site can support Unicode, this should be corrected for Spanish polish.
- `animate-pulse` on "Reproduciendo boceto..." feels more utility-app than premium showroom.
- The contact form submit is an anchor, so Enter in a field does not send the WhatsApp message through normal form submission.

#### Questions to Consider

- What if AREA LRMQ used the logo geometry as the visual system, rather than a generic premium serif mood?
- Should the desktop narrative prioritize showroom pacing, or should visitors be able to scan faster once they know what they need?
- Is the collection section trying to sell categories, or prove curation? Those need different layouts.
